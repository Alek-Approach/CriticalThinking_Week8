1Explain Yourself

Study

Activity 1 of 7 • Handout A Part 1 • Pairs

How would you describe these things and their sensations to someone who has never seen or experienced them before? Use all your senses in your descriptions!

  • A bunch of flowers
  • An automobile
  • A rainstorm
  • A laptop computer
  • A piece of chocolate
  • A rock concert
  • A hug from someone
  • A favourite place of yours
  1. Secretly choose 2 items. Make notes using all five senses: sight, sound, smell, taste and touch.
  2. Partner A describes one WITHOUT naming it. Partner B guesses. Then swap. Play 2 rounds each.
  3. Class debrief: which descriptions were guessed fastest? YOU name the strategies that worked.

Try these frames (Handout A)

  • “It’s the kind of thing you use for…”
  • “It’s about the size of a…”
  • “It feels…, and it makes a … sound.”
  • “You’d find it in a…”

Describe it for someone who has never seen or felt it before.

Can’t decide? Draw a mystery item

Don’t show your partner!

2Creativity × Critical Thinking

Engage

Activity 2 of 7 • Handout A Part 2 • Trios → class

Creativity opens

generates many ideas • asks ‘what if?’ • combines • imagines • says YES

Critical thinking tests

checks it works • finds weak points • compares • judges • asks WHY
  1. Sort the 9 statements below: Creativity, Critical or Both?
  2. Argue: pick one your trio disagrees on and argue it out. That disagreement is the real learning.
  3. Share: each trio reports its toughest card. The class votes.

Sorting cards (Handout A Part 2)

    There are no trick answers. Most cards can be argued as BOTH — and that’s the point: creativity opens up ideas, critical thinking tests them. The best innovators switch between the two on purpose.

    Hard to argue against: 7 and 9 are BOTH. Ready for debate: 1, 3 and 5 lean creative; 2, 4, 6 and 8 lean critical. Can your trio prove the opposite?

    3Sketch Them Out

    Activate

    Activity 3 of 7 • Handout B Part 1 • Individual → groups Assessed

    1. Everything around you — cars, buildings, even your phone — started with an idea. Start from a ‘what if’: What if a ___ could also ___? Choose an idea from the list or invent your own.
    2. Sketch it on Handout B Part 1. Boxes, arrows and labels are perfect. Drawing skill is NOT assessed — the idea is.
    3. Add your invention’s name, the problem it solves and your ‘what if’.
    4. Present it to your group in 1 minute. Each listener asks one curious question.

    Invention ideas

    • A portable source for charging a smartphone
    • A traffic system that doesn’t rely on colours
    • A machine that turns trash into energy
    • A “green” vehicle
    • An all-new method of personal transportation
    • A new immersive gaming system
    • An idea for a tablet or smartphone app
    • A challenging and fun new idea for a sport
    • A clever design for an interior space
    • An idea for a social networking site

    Handout B Part 1 is collected — it is part of your assessment.

    4Train of Tall Tales

    Engage

    Activity 4 of 7 • Circles of 5–6

    1. Sit in a circle. Everyone gets a unique picture card: a person, place, object or animal.
    2. One person starts a story with ONE sentence, using their picture as the focus.
    3. Going around, each person adds ONE sentence related to their own picture. Keep it moving — speed beats perfection!
    4. Round 2: draw a ‘what if’ card. You MUST work its twist into your sentence. Then tell the class your best moment.

    ‘What if’ twist cards

    • …gravity switched off?
    • …the animals could talk?
    • …it all happened backwards?
    • …everyone forgot one word?

    Creative: connect things that don’t belong. Critical: keep the story making sense.

    Draw a twist card

    5What Makes a Pitch Work?

    Study

    Activity 5 of 7 • Handout B Part 2 • Pairs → class

    Weak pitch

    “Um, so I made a cup. It’s a cup that, like, keeps your coffee not too hot. I think it’s good. Yeah. You could maybe use it. That’s it.”

    Strong pitch

    “Ever burned your tongue on a coffee that’s TOO hot — then waited so long it went cold? Meet the GoldiCup: it holds your drink at the perfect temperature for a full hour. One button, no ice, no waiting. It’s the smart cup for people who are too busy to babysit their coffee.”

    1. With your partner: what does the strong pitch DO that the weak one doesn’t? List it on Handout B Part 2.
    2. Build the class checklist from YOUR answers.

    Our class checklist

    Type each idea the class agrees on. You’ll tick these off in Activity 6.

      6Your 60-Second Elevator Pitch

      Activate

      Activity 6 of 7 • Handout B Part 3 • Individual → class Assessed

      This exercise is used in business to help you think fast and get your point across. Convince your listener in the time it takes to ride an elevator: about 30 to 60 seconds. Choose and use your words carefully and persuasively!

      1. Your topic: the invention you sketched in Activity 3. Make quick notes, then write your pitch on Handout B Part 3.
      2. Rehearse with a partner and time each other.
      3. Deliver it to the class. Stand tall, speak up, believe your idea!
      4. Listeners give one GLOW and one GROW in the Handout B feedback box.

      Pitch frames (Handout A)

      • Hook: “Have you ever struggled to…? My invention solves that.”
      • What it is: “It’s a … that lets you…”
      • Why it’s different: “Unlike anything today, it…”
      • Memorable close: “In one sentence: it’s the … for…”

      Feedback: GLOW “What I loved was…” • GROW “One thing that could be even stronger is…”
